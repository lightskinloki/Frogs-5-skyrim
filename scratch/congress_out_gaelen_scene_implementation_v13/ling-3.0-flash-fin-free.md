## The Root-Chamber Protocol

### Frame for the GM

This scene is not a negotiation. It is a room full of people trying to stop a machine, and the machine's operator is standing in the middle of the room talking to each of them individually about why they should stop trying. The room has a clock. The clock is the ritual.

Gaelen is not the GM's opponent. He is the GM's most dangerous tool — because everything he says is true, and the truth is what makes the players hesitate.

The GM runs this scene in three passes: **Establishment**, **Stations**, and **The Question**. Between the stations, the ritual clock advances. The players' choices drive everything. Rolls, if they happen at all, are rare and narrow.

---

### PASS ONE: ESTABLISHMENT

**The GM reads the room aloud.** The undercroft. The root-column. The obsidian array blooming inward. The crimson-vested figure at the center, his grafted arm turning a shard. The Daedric script on the floor repeating: *THE TOWER TOUCHES ALL THE MANTLES OF HEAVEN*.

Gaelen opens. He does not threaten. He does not posture. He speaks the way a doctor speaks to a patient who doesn't yet know they're sick — with calm, with evidence, with the quiet certainty of someone who has already run the numbers. His opening is the source monologue or a paraphrase of it (new mythic dawn leader, §VI). He gestures at the root, at the sky, at the sky's laws. He says: you are exhausted. You are bleeding to hold together a system designed to break. Let me show you what I have found.

**Key GM move:** Gaelen does not single anyone out yet. He speaks to the room as a room. The players will naturally respond — someone will step forward, someone will stay back, someone will reach for a weapon. He waits. He does not rush. He is genuinely interested in whatever the table does.

**If a player attacks him directly:** He does not retaliate. He does not even stop working the shard his arm is turning. He says something like: "I hoped you would. It means you're listening." Then he keeps talking, and the shard keeps turning. The attack changes nothing about the ritual's progress. It only tells him the player's posture.

**If a player addresses him directly:** He stops what he's doing — just the working of the shard, not the whole ritual — and turns to them. This is the opening of a station.

---

### PASS TWO: THE STATIONS

The stations are not pre-scripted encounters. They are **moments that occur when a player steps into Gaelen's attention or when the ritual's pressure reaches a threshold.** The GM decides which station fires based on what the table does, not on a predetermined sequence.

#### Station Structure

Each station has three components:
1. **The address** — Gaelen speaks to a specific person, drawing on what he knows about them (new mythic dawn leader, §VI Key Dialogue). This is a scripted-lite moment: the GM has the line ready but delivers it based on what the player actually does and says.
2. **The personal stake** — what Gaelen's words touch in that character. This is drawn from the character's established wound (GEAR's unresolved existence, Varon's pain and imposed identity, Ylva's critique of judgment, Esbern's opposition, Bjorn's grief, Mila's protectiveness).
3. **The companion reaction** — another NPC companion responds visibly to what Gaelen just said. This is a GM-only beat. The GM describes what the companion does — wavers, hardens, speaks, stays silent — based on what Gaelen said and what the companion's arc currently is.

**How Gaelen chooses who to address (proposed design):**

Gaelen does not pick strategically. He addresses whoever has done one of three things:
- **Stepped forward** physically or verbally into his space
- **Asked him a question** (even a hostile one — he regards questions as engagement)
- **Been named or referenced by another player** (if Alfonso's player says "what about you?" Gaelen may turn to Alfonso even if Alfonso hasn't spoken)

He also, crucially, **responds to what the ritual is doing.** If an anchor is close to completing, he may address whoever is working on anchors: "You are close. Do you know what you are close to?" He ties his words to the physical reality of the room.

He does NOT address everyone at once. He addresses one person per station. The station ends when that person has responded — or when the ritual clock advances and something else demands attention.

**The Ylva Exchange (special station):**

This fires when Ylva has been in the room and Gaelen has spoken about rules, nature, or freedom. It is a scripted exchange (FIRE_B_module.md §22, line 1915):

> **GAELEN:** "Rules are the bars, hunter. I am unbending them."
> **YLVA:** "The rules are what make the hunt HOLY. Prey runs, hunter chases, the fastest wins -- that is the whole church. You are not freeing anything. You are pissing on the game."

Ylva speaks for the theologically serious objection. She is the most theologically equipped person in the room to say why the Unmaking is wrong. She is not arguing against Gaelen out of loyalty to the party — she is arguing because her whole identity as a hunter depends on the rules Gaelen wants to erase. The GM does not explain this at the table. The line is enough.

**The Alfonso Duel (special station):**

This fires when Alfonso (carried by Orion, or awake and present) has been addressed or has addressed Gaelen. It is a scripted-lite beat (FIRE_B_module.md §22, line 1916):

> **GAELEN:** "You of all things know the cage, brother. Rot is the door. Help me open it."

Gaelen finds the rapier in the room without being shown it. He addresses Alfonso directly. This is the only person Gaelen speaks to who might actually have a personal stake in what he's offering — Alfonso already moved his soul into a sword. The GM gives Alfonso's player the moment and lets them answer it live. Whatever they say, and however long they take, is theirs.

**The Hircine Temptation (ambient, not a station):**

This does not fire as a choice. It fires as ambient pressure. Just before or during the disruption fight, Saijah may hear the Hunter's voice: "Little sentinel. That noise in your skull. I can make it STOP." The GM does not present this as a choice. It hangs in the air. The channel that screams is the same channel Kynareth speaks through (the Mzinchaleft principle: silence is not always peace). If the player accepts outright, the GM does not block it — but that is the player firing a crossroad early, not the module's design. The sensation may sharpen a moment (a taste), and the offer hangs.

### THE NPC CONVICTION TRACK (proposed design)

**This is a GM-side tracking tool. Players never roll to make their NPCs waver.** The GM tracks each NPC companion's position on a simple three-state scale:

- **STEADFAST** — Opposed to Gaelen, defending the ritual or the party's current course
- **WAVERING** — Listening, uncertain, something Gaelen said landed
- **OPEN** — Leaning toward Gaelen's offer, or actively arguing for it

**How positions shift (proposed design):**

A companion shifts state when one of these happens:
1. **Gaelen addresses them directly** (the station address). The GM decides the shift based on what Gaelen said and what the companion's arc is.
2. **They observe the party's response** to a station. If the party dismisses Gaelen, a wavering companion may harden. If the party hesitates, a steadfast companion may waver.
3. **They observe the ritual's effects** (the anchors falling, the weather worsening, the Time-Wound's pressure). Visible suffering creates doubt; visible destruction creates resolve.
4. **A player talks to them** about what just happened. The player's character's words matter more than Gaelen's.

**Tracking method (proposed design):**

The GM keeps a simple note:

| Companion | Starting State | Shift Triggers | Current State |
|-----------|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| Esbern | STEADFAST | Blades vs. Mythic Dawn | STEADFAST |
| Bjorn | STEADFAST | Gaelen's offer = end of grief | WAVERING |
| Mila | STEADFAST | Concrete, protective | STEADFAST |
| Ylva | WAVERING | Gaelen's rules critique | WAVERING |
| Varon | WAVERING | Pain, imposed identity | OPEN |
| GEAR | WAVERING | Recognition of own nature | OPEN |
| Davinia | WAVERING | Ismara's influence, identity | STEADFAST |
| Orion | STEADFAST | Valerius's control | STEADFAST |
| Nora | STEADFAST | Horn-song, courage | STEADFAST |
| Saijah | STEADFAST | Kynareth's voice | WAVERING |
| Alfonso | WAVERING | Already outside the body | OPEN |

**Starting states are set before the session. Shifts happen in real time as the scene runs. The GM announces shifts openly — "Esbern's jaw sets. He doesn't agree with any of this, but he's listening" — or keeps them silent, depending on what serves the table.**

The GM does NOT read the table the states. The GM decides based on what happened. The table is a memory aid, not a rule.

### THE RITUAL CLOCK (proposed design)

**The clock is the pressure mechanism. It runs in real game time, not game time.** The GM sets a mental timer. Each anchor takes approximately 3-5 minutes of real time to complete (the GM determines the pace based on how long the social scene has been running).

**Anchor progress is tracked visibly.** The GM describes the anchor-shards as they complete:

- **First anchor down:** The red light in the channels beneath the floor dims slightly. The root pulses once, weaker. The weather in the chamber shifts — Zone 3 stays Zone 3, but the alarm interval shortens slightly (from ~6 min to ~5 min).
- **Second anchor down:** The sap-lines wither visibly. Black veins in the root-column recede. The alarm interval shortens further (~4 min). The secondary horror table gains one additional entry (the GM adds a flavor table: a memory of the anchor's last conscious moment, heard in the stone).
- **Third anchor down:** THE DISRUPTION POINT. The whole undercroft lurches. The red light gutters. Gaelen phases (FIRE_B_module.md §22, lines 1943-1953). The scene transitions to the aftermath.
- **Fourth and fifth anchors down:** The ritual is staunched. Arngeir comes down. Rewards. But Gaelen is gone — he reached for a second prize and escaped.

**How the clock advances:**

The GM does not say "two minutes have passed." The clock advances through **descriptive beats.** Every few lines of dialogue or every few player actions, the GM inserts a ritual-beat:

> The shard under Gaelen's grafted arm shifts. A bead of black sap wells up at its edge. The chanting in the chamber — faint, rhythmic, coming from the assistants at the array's edge — continues without pause.

This is the pressure. The players can hear it. They can feel it. But they cannot stop it by talking — only by destroying anchors.

**If the players focus entirely on social interaction and ignore the anchors:** The GM lets the ritual advance. At three anchors down, it's over — Gaelen phases, the scene ends, and the party has lost the opportunity to disrupt the working. This is the failure state. The GM does not railroad it. The players chose to talk, and the machine finished its work.

### THE COMPANION REACTIONS (proposed design)

**NPCs are not spectators.** They have their own agendas and will act on them regardless of what the players do. The GM runs them as active participants in the scene, not as buttons to press.

**Steadfast companions** will:
- Argue with Gaelen directly (Esbern: "Mythic Dawn. You're the same order that murdered the Emperor.")
- Physically position themselves between Gaelen and an anchor (Bjorn might step toward the array)
- Ask the party to act ("We don't have time for this — the anchors, now")
- Stay silent and watch, waiting for the players to make the first move

**Wavering companions** will:
- Ask Gaelen a question (not to challenge him, but because they genuinely want to understand)
- Look at the party for validation ("What do you think?")
- Physically hesitate — half-turned toward Gaelen, half-toward the party
- Say something that cuts both ways

**Open companions** will:
- Argue *for* Gaelen against the party (Varon: "You don't understand what you're carrying. I do.")
- Try to protect Gaelen or the ritual from the party's aggression
- Speak as if Gaelen's offer is already settled ("He's right, you know. He's been right all along.")

**Important rule (proposed design):** A companion who goes OPEN does not immediately betray the party. They argue for Gaelen, they try to protect the ritual, they may physically stand in the party's way — but they do not switch sides and fight against the party unless physically compelled. They are persuaded, not possessed. The party can still talk them down.

**Talking them down (proposed design):** A player can attempt to talk a wavering or open companion back to the party's side. This is a **Guile roll against Standard (+0) difficulty** — not a persuasion roll against the companion, but a roll to see whether the player's character can reach them in this moment. The GM does not announce the roll as a "persuasion check." The player says what they want to do, and the GM calls for the roll if the outcome is genuinely uncertain. A failure means the companion is not reached *yet* — they may be reachable later. A critical success means the companion has a moment of clarity and acts decisively.

### PASS THREE: THE QUESTION

After the stations have fired and the ritual clock has reached a natural pause point — but **before** three anchors are down — Gaelen poses the question. This is the Torygg moment. It is the final pure-roleplay beat.

**The Question (proposed design):**

Gaelen stops working. Not the ritual — the whole thing. He stands before the array and looks at the party. He does not argue. He does not plead. He asks.

> "You have heard what I have to say. You have seen what I am. You know what I am offering. What do you choose?"

This is not a skill check. There is no roll. There is no difficulty modifier. This is the moment where the table has to answer for itself. Gaelen waits. He will wait as long as the table needs.

**The Question has no single answer.** The party may:
- Agree with Gaelen and commit to the Unmaking
- Refuse and commit to stopping the ritual
- Split — some agreeing, some refusing
- Ask for time, or ask a clarifying question
- Do nothing, and let the ritual clock decide

**If the party agrees with Gaelen (proposed design):**

Agreement is not the end of the scene. It is the beginning of a different conversation. The GM does not end the scene because the party said yes. Instead:

1. **What do they agree to?** Terms matter. What do they give up? What do they keep? The GM asks the table this openly.
2. **The NPC companions still have to reckon with it.** A companion who was wavering may now be confirmed — but a steadfast companion who opposed the agreement will have to live with it. The GM describes their reactions: Ismara's silence, Esbern's jaw, Bjorn's look, Nora's hands. They are not won over. They are present.
3. **The ritual does not stop just because they agreed.** The anchors are still there. The weather is still worsening. Agreement means the party is now *choosing* to let the ritual continue — which means they are choosing to let the anchors finish. The GM asks: do they let the anchors complete, or do they agree to Gaelen's terms and then still try to control the outcome? This is the agency question. Saying yes to Gaelen does not mean saying yes to everything he wants.
4. **Jasper's response.** If the party agreed, Jasper watches them. He does not attack. He simply waits to see what they do next. He is content with his choice. They do not have to be.

### TRANSITION TO COMBAT (proposed design)

**Combat begins when the threshold is crossed.** The threshold is: a player takes physical action against an anchor, the ritual, Jasper, or an assistant.

**What counts as crossing the threshold:**
- Wrenching an anchor-shard free (Hard Might -4 attempt)
- Casting a spell at the array or a shard
- Physically striking Jasper
- Attacking a Dawn assistant who is working the ritual
- Smashing or damaging any part of the spiral array

**What does NOT count:**
- Pointing a weapon at Gaelen (he does not retaliate)
- Threatening Gaelen verbally
- Drawing a weapon without acting
- Any action directed at Gaelen himself

**When the threshold is crossed:**

The social scene ends. The GM announces: "The room changes." The ritual clock accelerates — the alarm fires immediately, and the Zone 3 tempo rules apply in full. The anchors become contested objects. Jasper and the assistants become enemies. Gaelen continues to work the ritual, talking, but he does not fight. He defends the work, not himself.

The GM then runs the disruption fight as described in FIRE_B_module.md §22 (lines 1926-1953): 5 anchor-shards, 25 HP each, DR 10, Hard Might (-4) or Hard Magic (-4) to destroy, three down = Gaelen phases, Jasper fights to the death.

**If the party never crosses the threshold:**

The ritual completes. Gaelen phases at three anchors down. The scene ends with the aftermath — Arngeir and the Greybeards arrive to staunch what they can, the Way of the Voice opens, and Gaelen is gone, curious about the party now, with a grievance attached. This is not a failed scene. It is a completed scene with consequences. The party talked, and the machine finished its work. They can still pursue the Eldergleam campaign later.

### HOW TO RUN THIS AT THE TABLE — PRACTICAL NOTES

**Before the session:**
- Read the NPC conviction tracking table above and set starting states for each companion
- Have Gaelen's personal addresses memorized (the Key Dialogue from the NPC doc)
- Have the Ylva and Alfonso scripted beats ready but flexible
- Set the anchor pacing (3-5 minutes real time per anchor is a starting point; adjust based on how long the social scene runs at your table)

**During the scene:**
- Start with the read-aloud. Let the room speak for itself before Gaelen speaks.
- Gaelen waits. Do not rush his opening. Let silence do work.
- After the opening, **watch the table.** Who steps forward? Who stays back? Who speaks? Who is silent? This tells you which station fires next.
- When a station fires, deliver Gaelen's address (from memory or the doc), then describe the companion reaction, then let the player respond.
- Insert ritual-beats between social beats. The clock must be visible.
- Track companion states silently. Announce shifts only when they matter.
- The Question fires when the table has had enough engagement with the stations AND the ritual clock hasn't reached three anchors yet. You'll feel it.

**If the table stalls:**
- Advance the ritual clock. Describe an anchor shifting, a shard cracking audibly, a sap-line bursting.
- Have a companion react to the advancing clock — Esbern says "we're running out of time," Bjorn shifts his weight toward the array, Nora's gems flicker.
- Do not narrate urgency as a timer. Narrate it as the room changing around them.

**If a player tries to meta-game (e.g., "how do I persuade my NPC?"):**
- Remind them: there is no persuasion roll against an NPC's conviction. There is a Guile roll to see if your character can reach them in this moment, but the GM decides the shift based on what happened, not the roll.
- The roll is for genuine uncertainty. If the player says "I try to talk Varon down," and Varon is currently wavering, the GM can say "roll Guile — but I already know the answer, so tell me what you say and I'll tell you what happens" — the roll is formality, not gate.

### THE ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE (proposed design)

The Blue Palace sequence works because each encounter tests a different axis — mettle, discretion, judgment, identity — and the final question asks for all of it at once. This scene works the same way, but the axes are **personal** rather than political.

Each station tests a different companion's relationship to Gaelen's offer:
- **Saijah:** Can she hear Kynareth through the pain, or does the Hunter's silence sound like peace?
- **Alfonso:** Does being outside the body make him more or less capable of choosing?
- **Ylva:** Are the rules sacred because they make the hunt holy, or are they bars?
- **GEAR:** Does a machine have a soul, or can it learn to want?
- **Varon:** Does a face that isn't yours mean you don't have to carry its pain?
- **Bjorn:** Does ending grief mean ending the ones you love?
- **Esbern:** Can a Blade serve an ancient enemy without becoming one?
- **Mila:** Can she protect the people she loves from something that's making them whole?

The ritual clock makes each of these urgent. The anchor-shards make each of these consequential. The companion reactions make each of these communal — one person's wavering changes the room for everyone.

And the Question at the end asks all of it: not "do you believe Gaelen?" but "what do you choose, knowing what you know, with everyone you care about watching?"

---

### WHAT THIS IS NOT (proposed design)

- It is not a social-combat system. There is no "persuasion vs. resistance" mechanic. There is no damage dealt by words. There is no HP pool for Gaelen's arguments.
- It is not a skill-check gauntlet. Rolls, if they happen, are narrow and situational — a Guile roll to reach a wavering companion, a Might roll to wrench an anchor. The social scene itself is not gated by rolls.
- It is not a monologue. Gaelen speaks, but the scene is driven by what the table does. The GM has prepared lines and beats, but the order and timing are responsive.
- It does not force a conclusion. The party can agree, refuse, split, or stall — and each path has consequences. The GM does not punish any choice. The GM makes sure every choice has weight.
