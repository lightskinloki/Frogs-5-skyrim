## 1. The shape: four kinds of test, one room

Map the Blue Palace skeleton directly onto this room, because the brief asks for it and it already fits:

1. **A test of position** (Tullius) → Gaelen's opening address + the Daedric script evidence in the players' own hands (`FIRE_B_module.md:1909`).
2. **Multiple independent conversations** (Whispering Court) → the party mingles inside a hostile room: assistants, Jasper, and their own companions will all talk to them, all sincerely.
3. **A live consequence for standing still** (Thalmor) → an NPC acts on their own while the party is still talking. Doing nothing has a visible cost.
4. **A final pure-roleplay question** (Torygg) → the terminal ask. No roll, ever.

Gaelen is not Tullius — he is not testing them. But the *structural* job of each encounter transfers: distinct kinds of pressure in one room, resolved by choice, ending in a question nobody can roll against.

## 2. Scene open — frame once, then stop

- Read-aloud (`:1894-1907`) as written. Position the room: Gaelen at the root working the spiral; Jasper working and watching; assistants at the anchors **[P: 2–3 Dagonite Cultists + 1 Shard Zealot, reusing the module's existing stat cards — the cards exist at `:911-913,995`; Scene 22 as written lists only Gaelen and Jasper as enemies, while `:1930` already assumes "the cultists cheat it," so assistants are canon-implied, not statted]**; the company at the threshold with Balthazzar.
- **State the threshold out loud, once, in-fiction** (Jasper or an assistant says it, plainly) and then never re-explain it: *first hostile physical action against an anchor, the working, Jasper, or an assistant — or a companion actually taking up the work — and this stops being a conversation.* The GM's locked triggers (`PREP:16-19`) are the specific cases; this sentence is the umbrella the table agrees to.
- Show the ledger before it matters: **the ritual clock** and **the companion marks** are both invisible to players except through description.

## 3. The pressure engine: the alarm is the clock

Reuse the mechanic the module already locks, don't invent one.

**[P] The Ritual Clock — three stages, one alarm each.**
Set a hidden real-world alarm at scene open (canon interval ~6 min, `:753`; `:755` explicitly permits adjusting it). On each alarm during the talk phase:

1. Advance the clock one stage (stage 3 = lock).
2. Read one concrete physical change — prepared as three short lines, in this order: **stage 1** the black line reaches the base of the first anchor; **stage 2** halfway up the obsidian, ripples starting to leak past the chamber mouth; **stage 3** the three keystones begin to answer each other (from Jasper's own journal, `chapter 30:245` — the party has read this, so the image is theirs, not the GM's invention).
3. At stage 3, **shorten the alarm** (canon dial: higher pressure = more frequent alarm, `:745`). Then fire the terminal ask.

Rules that make it honest:
- **Talking never slows the clock.** Only physical interference does. The trade the players are making is *information versus time*, and that trade has to be real or the scene has no pressure.
- **The alarm does not care that they are mid-sentence.** That is the mechanic (`:755`: the alarm and the dread are the actual mechanic).
- No Tempo rolls during the talk phase (Tempo is combat-only, `:745` step 3), so no aging and no secondary horror until steel comes out. The social phase stays clean; the weather is the fight's problem.
- Every alarm also buys one line of visible assistant labor — a shard turned, the chant continuing — so the room is always working while they talk.

**[P] Re-entrancy.** If they cross the threshold and fight, and then the fighting stops (anchors down, Jasper dead, assistants down), the loop resumes with whatever stage the clock reached. Talk → fight → talk is legal. Combat is a mode, not a verdict.

## 4. The loop: one exchange

The unit of play is an **exchange**, not a round and not a turn. Unlimited exchanges fit inside one alarm interval; the alarm, not the exchange count, drives the clock.

1. **Address.** Whoever's player speaks, speaks — players always have the queue. If no player takes it, **Gaelen picks one target** (rules in §5).
2. **Answer.** The addressed person answers. Player-run characters answer by their player. GM-run companions answer by the GM using their one-line disposition (§6). Assistants and Jasper answer honestly if asked — the room has no liars in it; that is the scene's signature.
3. **Mark.** Apply at most one mark (§6).
4. **Live detail.** One physical line: where Gaelen's graft is, what an assistant is doing, what the sap is doing. Action only — no motive narration, per Writing Guide IX.0.

**Seeded beats, dropped into whichever exchange fits** (all canon text already in the module; the GM reads them, I am not writing them):
- The Ylva exchange, both liturgies, one beat, out loud (`:1915`).
- **Alfonso's address goes to his player to answer live, however long he takes** (`:1916` — "give his player the moment").
- The Mythic Dawn reply if a player names the order (`:1917`).
- The Jasper reveal routed through Gaelen, with recognition presented to Davinia's player first (`:1937`) — noting `PREP:25`: Davinia is fronting and **has not read the journal**, so the recognition lands on top of a name the party knows and she does not.
- Hircine's station at the doorway of the boss (`:1919`): fires at stage 1 or on the first alarm, offer hanging, no weredog offer, no resolution.
- The Quiet Wish lands **during the fight** (`PREP:20-22`) — do not spend it in the talk phase.

## 5. How Gaelen chooses and responds

**[P]** Five rules, improv-friendly, all consistent with his four pillars (`new mythic dawn leader:144-177`):

1. **One address per pick.** He is having quiet conversations with equals, not running a checklist.
2. **A player's question jumps the queue.** If someone asks him something, he answers it first, with total sincerity, before initiating anything new. He answers only what he actually knows; he does not dump lore the party hasn't asked for (Reasoning Pattern #19 — the GM's information is for writing *from*).
3. **Pick order:** whoever spoke to him last → otherwise the companion standing nearest the work who has not been addressed → otherwise the companion whose wound matches the point he is currently making (the matrix, §6).
4. **Never twice in a row on the same person; never twice on the same point.** If a player counters him with something true, he concedes the true part and moves on from there. Holding a position then conceding on merit (Pattern #4) is his characterization, not a debate tactic.
5. **He never initiates on someone bleeding, held, or a child.** Mila gets no address unless she forces one — not because she cannot be reasoned with (the existing scratch matrix's "ignored completely" line is a draft I would drop), but because he has no interest in a fight he would have to win by frightening her.

He does not persuade the players with mechanics. **There is no roll against Gaelen, and no roll for Gaelen.** He is not a bar. `GM Guide\Main Guide:31` covers the only exception: a Master-tier antagonist whose action is not being directly resisted is narrated succeeding — which here means his *working* proceeds, narrated, on the alarm.

## 6. NPC tracking: two boxes, no persuasion rolls

**[P] One private line per GM-run companion:**

```
GEAR     toward  [ ][ ]  ACT: takes up a place at the array
VARON    toward  [ ][ ]  ACT: none — hand to the scar
ESBERN   away    [ ][ ]  ACT: draws and moves on an anchor
YLVA     held    [ ][ ]  ACT: only on Alfonso's word
BJORN    steady  [ ][ ]  ACT: none — puts himself between Mila and the shards
MILA     steady  [ ][ ]  ACT: none — watches hands
NORA     neutral [ ][ ]  ACT: none
```

**Marks are earned by events, never by rolls:**
- Gaelen addresses them and **nobody counters it** → +1.
- A *witnessed fact* lands on them (Jasper whole and warm; the journal's signature; a companion's own answer) → +1.
- **[P]** A player gives them a real reason — an argument, a claim on them, a physical anchor (Alfonso to Ylva, Davinia to Bjorn, Orion to Nora, the handshake and "I've got you," `chapter 30:397-403`) → clear a mark.
- 1 mark = one visible tell, action only (a step, a hand, a silence). **2 marks = the ACT.**

The ACT is where the scene bites. **The act is always physical, never a converted opinion.** GEAR rolls toward the array and the clock jumps a stage. Esbern draws and advances on an anchor, and Jasper engages — meaning **the party's own NPC starts the fight** unless someone holds him. That is the Thalmor encounter's job in this room: standing still now has a cost, and the cost is their friend.

Two consequences worth having:
- Nobody defects *as a conclusion*. Drift shows up as a body moving, which the camera can film and the players can answer — never as a narrated change of heart.
- **Defection toward the working and hostility toward the party are different.** GEAR helping is not a combat trigger; stopping him with force is *their* choice, and force against a person starts initiative (§7). A companion can be talked down without a roll; restraining one physically is an Opposed Check (`Main Guide:21`).

**[P] Roll discipline — the complete list of rolls in this scene:**
1. Opposed checks for physical restraint (restraining Esbern/GEAR, or being restrained).
2. Anchor damage: 300 HP / DR 10; Orion's tonal counter-note, Hard Magic (−4), 100 damage, ignores DR (`PREP:7-11`).
3. A Guile roll **only** if the table agrees an attempt to move an NPC is genuinely uncertain — difficulty set by what is already on the table, against the player's own TN (`Main Guide:10-20`). Saijah rolls at −2 Guile in this zone (`:760`). A roll may settle *whether an action lands*; it never converts an NPC to Gaelen's side.
4. Nothing for the terminal ask. Nothing against Gaelen.

## 7. Transition to combat

Initiative starts on the first of the locked triggers (`PREP:16-19`), restated as one rule: **first hostile physical action against the ritual side, or a companion taking up the work and being met with force.** Once it starts:

- **Gaelen enters initiative but never as a combatant.** His turn is always: work the array (advances nothing new — the clock keeps its own time) and keep speaking. He is present in the order so the players cannot forget him, and harmless in it so the rule holds (`:1912`).
- Jasper fights to the death; assistants fight by belief and use non-lethal interposition against a person before steel if that is plausible **[P]**; tide-shard Tempo cheating applies as elsewhere (`:747`).
- Zone 3 Tempo, secondary horror, and the ~6-min alarm run as written (`:1930,753`).
- Weather softens one step per anchor down (`:1928`) — so the fight visibly gets *quieter* as they win, which is the mechanical arc and the story arc.
- At three anchors down: disruption, and Gaelen phases out exactly as written (`:1943-1952`).
- **De-escalation is legal:** if the party stops, and there is nothing left to defend against, the assistants go back to work and Gaelen keeps talking. Belief-driven protectors stop when the threat stops **[P]** — which also means killing Jasper removes the party's own escalation engine.

## 8. The terminal ask, and the three ways the scene ends

At stage 3, the keystones answer each other, and the loop terminates into **THE ASK**: Gaelen stops working, turns, and asks the party one question that has no roll attached. The players answer in their own words. This is Encounter 4 of the Blue Palace, mirrored — Torygg asked why they risk their lives; here the question is aimed at what they are protecting and what they will pay to keep it. **[P]** Do not script it; the GM already has everything needed to improvise it, because the answer he wants is the one the whole scene has been soliciting.

Three terminations, all legal:

1. **Refusal** → per `PREP:18-19`: he goes back to work and Jasper engages. Refusal is combat; that is what makes the ask load-bearing.
2. **Answer** → see §9.
3. **Silence / deflection** → he accepts it as an answer too, returns to the work, and the clock locks. The Fork is a failure to act, and acting is not required to choose (`scratch/SCENE_22…:31` — one line of that draft I would keep as-is).

## 9. If the party agrees: agency, priced and honored

**[P]** Agreement is a real branch, not a cutscene, and it must not be punished as a trick:

- **No hidden betrayal.** He means every word; if he accepts terms, the terms hold, because he is sincere (`new mythic dawn leader:134-137`).
- **The cost is visible before commitment.** The GM holds the ledger — gate 3 `:180-185` (Tower corrupted, temporal distortion spreading, Greybeards cut off, Dagon clock jumps, Saijah's migraine permanent) — and has already shown the room enough for the players to infer it. No auto-conclusions: the GM does not hand them the ledger; he has shown them the waystones, the masters' door, the weather.
- **Negotiation is open and answered honestly.** He will say what actually happens if they ask for limits. If the honest answer is "that is not how it works," they learn a fact instead of hitting a wall.
- **Their NPCs do not come along for free.** Run one resolution exchange per opposed companion — Esbern at minimum — on camera, with the party handling it. No roll to persuade. If a companion's own conviction is strong enough that they will not come, the party chooses whether to restrain them (Opposed Check) or let them go, and either is playable.
- **GEAR's question stays open.** Whoever runs GEAR gets the address; if the GM runs him, he moves on the clock and the party manages him. **The GM never answers GEAR's existential question for him** — the same Rule 1 discipline that governs PCs.
- **Downstream handoff:** Scene 23's read-aloud (`:1960-1963`) assumes disruption. Agreement needs a parallel branch written for it. That is a Scene 23 item, flagged here so it isn't discovered mid-session.
