# SCENE 21C — THE CHAINED STELE
## (Antechamber of the Earth-Bone / Lost Vault of the Return)

### Source-of-truth pass (G1 — cited before the build)

Building from the brief's own canon, quoted at the load-bearing points:

- **Nora's role lock:** *"Nora must NEVER explain the solution, lecture Orion, or auto-solve the puzzle. Nora's role is strictly to observe physical anomalies ... and ask intelligent, observant questions."* (brief §3)
- **Surveillance lock:** *"Valerius sees and hears everything Orion sees and hears through the Ring of the Pale Regent in real time... he hears every spoken word in the chamber as if he were standing right beside them."* (brief §2.3) — note this means **even pantomime and dust-scrawl are SEEN through his eyes**. The only truly private channels are Bal's whisper and Orion's own unspoken thoughts.
- **Bal's voice:** *"Cold, plain, commanding... Do not describe 'vibrations traveling through the marrow of vampire blood.'"* (brief §2.4)
- **The two truths** — Lordling (outlined in brief §4.1) and the Acoustic Counter-Tone (brief §4.2, the *bidirectional thread* + *concussive shockwave stunning the Sire*).
- **The stele text** — quoted verbatim in brief §4.3; used below as Nora's literal translation, unchanged.
- **Choice frame** — *"snap the leash now (blinding Valerius, risking immediate backlash) vs. biding time with Molag Bal's corruption."* (brief §6.4)

Per the no-tool-call mandate I did not re-open the repo this pass; the dialogue audit below runs against Writing Guide §IX Rules 0 and 7 as quoted in AGENTS.md, plus the brief's own named bans (no auto-solve, no purple, no mic-drop register, no NPC lectures). If a line survives that audit only loosely, kill it — the register rules are the strictest ones here.

## 1. SCENE STAGING & READ-ALOUD

**Staging notes.** This is Orion's vignette. Set the other Lanterns holding the seam of the basalt slab (off the stele, out of the room's acoustic geometry) unless the GM's recorded party composition says otherwise. The room is safe — no combat, no trap, no roll to enter. The stele was cut to be read by the *next* soul who needed it. Everything tense in this room comes out of conversation, not dice.

**Read-aloud** (from the doorway, once the party steps through):

> The chamber behind the basalt slab is colder than the mountain around it, and older. No draugr work here — no panels, no enamel, no tombs. Just raw stone cut flat, the knife-marks still sharp, and iron laid along the walls in long rusted runs from floor to ceiling, like the rigging of a ship hauled up to dry.
>
> A deep recess is cut into the far wall — deep enough that a tall man could stand inside it — and a slab of dark stone stands in it, grown rather than dragged, its grain running straight up like a frozen river. It is wrapped from base to crown in heavy iron chain, every link hauled flat and diamond-tight, and the chain that crosses its middle sings. Not loud. A continual thread of sound at the very floor of what an ear can catch — the way a harp string keeps speaking long after the hand has left it.
>
> Thumb-thick iron pegs run down both sides of the recess, their heads polished bright where countless hands have turned them. The chains that don't go to the pegs have their free ends hammered flat against the rock, killed. In the spaces between the binds, the slab's face is covered in short wedge-strokes, each no bigger than a fingernail, laid in rows like figures on a merchant's board. Around the lip of the recess, the same wedge-work runs in long, unbroken lines with no gaps — a fence drawn in one stroke.

**Nora's approach.** She walks straight up without being told — this is writing, her territory, and it is the one thing in the room she knows. Her beats, in sequence:

1. **Recognizes the script, not the text.** She runs a fingertip along the wedges. Genuine startled recognition at the *shape*, not its meaning.
2. **Translates the exposed passage** (the verbatim text from the brief) — slowly, haltingly, the way someone sounds out a language she's never heard spoken. Two places she stumbles: *"Bal's worm"* (she reads Bal's *name* aloud into the room) and the final line (the wolf-pack — she looks up at Orion at that one).
3. **The hum interrupts her.** Mid-translation she goes quiet, walks to the middle chain, and traces the air beside it without touching. Her observation: it's *in the iron*, not the air, and it's the *only* chain that sings — the others are dead silent.
4. **The bridge peg.** She notices the wear: the notch where a chain rides a peg is polished bright, but the iron itself isn't burnished there — the chain doesn't *slide* over the peg; the peg is *turned beneath* it. She tests one — a quarter-turn — and the pitch of the singing chain drops a step. She takes her hand off it fast.
5. **The near-slip, the interception, the coded recovery** — scripted in §2, below. This is where her hope collides with the surveillance state and she learns what it actually is.

## 2. NORA'S DIALOGUE — THE APPRENTICE PROMPTING THE TEACHER

Each line below supplies **one component** of the puzzle and **never** the synthesis. The synthesis is Orion's and nobody else's.

**Beat A — tentatively.**
> "These aren't letters. They're *strokes* — someone cut this stone like a tally of debts."

**Beat B — the translation.** Spoken like a child reading a foreign ledger aloud, stopping at the hard parts:
> "'Bal's worm whispers that the blood belongs to the father.'" *(A pause.)* "It says *the father*. Then: 'It is a thrall's lie.'"
> "...'Once the whelp has tasted dragon-meat and held his own hate, the blood answers only to the chest it beats in.'" *(She reads the lowest row twice with her finger under it.)* "'The whelp is no longer a child — he is a lord of his own wolf-pack.'"
> *(She looks up.)* "It names the worm's master. The stone means *that* Bal."

**Beat C — the hum.**
> "Quiet. Listen — this one. *(She walks to the middle chain, hand halting an inch from a link.)* It's not the air moving. It's the iron. One chain, and it's singing, and every other chain in the room is holding still. Why this one and not its neighbors?"

**Beat D — the bridge peg.**
> "These were turned. Look here — the notch is bright where the chain rides it, but the iron isn't worn there. The chain isn't sliding over this peg. The peg is being *turned* under the chain." *(She works one a quarter-turn. The singing chain drops a step.)*
> "You heard that. It went *lower*. I shouldn't have touched it — nothing in a room someone finished this carefully rings by accident."

**Beat E — the near-slip** (the danger beat). She's just read the wolf-pack line, and her hope tips out of her openly — in Valerius's hearing:
> "If the blood answers only to the chest it beats in, then it answers to *you*. It was never his —" *(She takes his ringed hand, turns the ring toward the light.)* "We could cut it away tonight. He'd never—"

**Orion's interception** — the player's move, and the mechanical heart of the beat. He speaks **over her**, room volume, in his teaching voice — the register she'd recognize as *her lessons*, and a stranger would read as a lecture. Suggested cover line the GM may hand the player:

> "A bound string answers its note — that's all a string is, a wire under a weight. You hold the note, not the wire. Hold it too hard and it snaps — and a snap travels the whole length of the string, right up to the peg that fastened it."

Three things this single sentence does:
- **To Nora:** "he's listening through the iron on your finger — hold the rest of the sentence."
- **To Valerius:** an acoustics lesson in a strange vault. **Unless he decodes it.** Every such line trips the ledger (§4).
- **To the design:** the line *says* the severance mechanic out loud in front of the ear that must not hear it. The player has to *want* this room to be dangerous to speak this truth. That is the tension asked for in brief §5.1.

**Nora learns the surveillance state — shown, never narrated (Rule 0).** No line like *"she understood"*. Instead: she goes still; her free hand closes over the ring on his finger and *squeezes once, hard*; she releases it and — after a moment — walks back to the stone. Her next words are her first deliberate act of indirection, born of exactly what she just learned:

**Beat F — the coded question that hands the floor to Orion.** She's recalling Beat D in the new register:
> "...So when the skald hammered the free chains flat — that's what that was. He killed the notes he didn't want sung."
> *(A breath. Quiet.)* "Orion. What note did he leave *singing*?"

She cannot answer this. It sounds like history-curiosity to an ear in the far room. To Orion it is the assembled puzzle: one live chain, four tuner pegs, dead chains where the maker ended what he didn't want. Her question is the entire handoff.

**Beat G — the craft passage.** If the player asks her to keep reading the lower rows, she translates — literally, without comprehension (this is a *document*, not an NPC's lecture, so routing truth through it violates nothing):
> "There's a passage below the figures. It's written like instructions — 'Sing the note the chain answers. Then sing its mirror. Where the two songs meet, the bond snaps, and the blow goes back down the road it came — to the maker.'" *(She frowns at her own translation.)* "It keeps saying 'the maker.' I don't know what it made."

That's the recipe. The *why* — sympathetic resonance, counter-phase, a bidirectional acoustic thread, and the snapback onto Orion at the near end — is stated in no document in the room. It has to come out of Orion's mouth.

## 3. PLAYER AGENCY & DIAGNOSTIC PROMPT

### The floor handoff (GM reads this verbatim to Orion's player)

> Orion has two questions on the table and a whole room holding its breath on him. You may go to the stone now, or ask Nora to keep reading the lower rows first:
>
> **1.** What was this room *built to do* — to the thing it was bound to? And what note did the skald leave singing?
>
> **2.** The true question you've both been circling since the word "wolf" came out of the stone: what is that singing bound up around, sitting on your own finger?
>
> Say what Orion hears, or sing to find out. If you sing the Diagnostic Song, tell me which chain you sing at first. You hold the note.

**Lockouts (brief §3):** Nora is *not* allowed to connect the pegs to the chain to the note. If the player tries to have her explain "the counter-tone," the GM refuses kindly: she knows the note changed and not why — she is the apprentice in this room and says so if pushed. Nora auto-solving is the discarded draft; it doesn't come back through a back door.

### Mechanics (FROGS 5 — roll *under* the stat; modifiers subtract; never a fixed DC)

The GM pulls Orion's acoustic stat from the sheet (the Diagnostic Song, learned watching Hjolmar Ice-Shaper — his own earned skill, not a blessing). Modifier ladder, applied sequentially:

| Step | Action | Roll | Success grants |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sing the room. Map the rig | Standard (TN = stat) | Isolates the one live chain; reads the peg bank as a *tuner grid*; reads the killed chains as deliberate silence — "this room was tuned for a single note." |
| 2 | Sing the live chain | Standard | Hears the chain is **double-strung**: its own voice *and* an answering voice from a long way off — the tether. Names the note that is the ring's. |
| 3 | Find the mirror (requires the player to have **stated** the theory in his own words first) | Hard (−4) | Confirms the unweaving note and its cost: the blow runs **both** ways; the Sire stuns at the far end; Orion's own hearing eats the snapback. |
| 4 | Execute (only if the player chooses it) | Hard (−4) | See Branch A. |

**Call-out rule:** Step 3 is not offered until the player *says how he thinks it works*. The mechanism is deduction, not dice; the roll only confirms the note and prices the consequences. If the player acts on Step 2 without ever stating the theory, the GM applies Hard (−4) to the execution and notes it.

**Truth 1 — the Lordling.** Once the stone's line (Nora's own translation) and the ring's answering note are both on the table, the GM states it plainly: *the ring is a real tether and a real eye, but the chain it holds Orion by is psychological. He is no child of the bloodline now — he fed alone, survived alone, held his own hate. The leash holds because he keeps playing the thrall.* This is canon, delivered as narrated truth at the correct moment — not as a puzzle for Nora.

**Truth 2 — the Acoustic Counter-Tone.** Only from Orion's mouth: a sympathetic tether is a bidirectional thread in the metal; sing its own note and the ring answers; sing its mirror and the thread snaps, sending a concussive shock up the length onto the Sire. He must also voice the cost *he* pays. That is the player's achievement in the scene. The stele never says it.

## 4. THE DAEDRIC CROSSFIRE

### Molag Bal's whisper — exact scripting (cold, plain, commanding; delivered as a voice in his head; the only channel in the room Valerius cannot touch)

**Entry (B1) — right when the wolf-pack line lands, as the option first forms in Orion.**

> You are already counting the moments until you tear it off. Stop.
>
> The ring is his leash on you. It is also his harness on himself — the one rope he has looped through your finger. Cut it and you cut the only line you hold on him.
>
> He is watching through it. Let him watch. Be a good dog to him, whelp. A man who spends his eye on a dog learns nothing while the dog is learning to hold the rope.

**Entry (B2) — when Orion considers the backlash cost, or shows the hesitation of fear.**

> The stone your girl is reading was cut by a skald who fed my broods to Coldharbour before the Dragon Cult tidied the world. What did he hold? Not a war-band. A song — and the nerve to sing it at a thing larger than he was. You have that same nerve. Use it on the one you actually owe.
>
> The Original Soul Trap — what I first gave my children, before the mortals softened it into the whimper that fills their gems. The true rite is a defilement. You take what is claimed, and what you take feeds Coldharbour, and it feeds the hand that took it. That is what a lord does with a leash. He pulls it.

**Branch closers** — whichever way the choice goes:

- Severs: **"You have chosen a blind road. I am patient. When he finds you — and he will find you — the offer still stands."**
- Bides: **"The whelp learns. Now keep him watching, and watch what he watches. Tomorrow I begin to show you what dies loudest."**

**GM delivery notes.** Speak Bal flat, unhurried, without theatricality. He does not warn, plead, or reassure — he prices things. He never names the ring's severance "wrong because it frees you"; he names it wrong because it *blindfolds* Orion against the day Bal wants him braced.

### The surveillance ledger (how the GM tracks spoken vs. unspoken)

Three channels, adjudicated per line:

- **HEARD** — every spoken word, *including whispers*. Read back instantly: "the chamber has ears. That line left the room."
- **SEEN** — everything in Orion's line of sight: Nora's face, gesture-code, dust-scrawl, the ring in the light. Valerius sees it *live*. There is no safe hidden-language channel through the body.
- **PRIVATE** — Molag Bal's whisper and Orion's own unspoken thoughts. The only two lanes Valerius physically cannot reach.

**The threshold (brief §5.3):** open discussion of severing the tether triggers retaliation — give it to Valerius *after* the scene (as a hunt, a question, a coldly different tone next contact). Coded lines (Beat F, the cover line) do not auto-trigger; they accumulate as **decode flags**. When flags accrue, Valerius later asks innocent questions about "what the girl read off the stones" — the GM resolves the decode off-screen and plays the consequence, so the fear carries without in-scene fiat. **Hard rule from the brief:** the ring gives *no* tell — no warmth, no pulse, no watchfulness. The dread is purely what the characters know.

### The choice, framed for the player

Present it as the GM, plainly, both costs named:

> The room has given you everything it has. One way: sing the mirror note into the chain, and the ring on your hand dies, and the eye on the far end of the thread is struck blind — and you eat the snap yours. The blow runs both ways. From this hour Valerius sees nothing you see, hears nothing you say — and he will *know* that he has been blinded, and hunt the cause. The two of you get air. You pay for it in a rolling hunt.
>
> The other way: keep the ring on your finger where he put it, keep being the good dog, and take Bal's tutelage — the Soul Trap — while the eye above you spends itself watching a servant who is already learning what dies loudest. You get the corruption. It works by *time*.

**Branch A — snap the leash (execution roll, Hard −4).** Success, clean: the chain's song breaks, the stone grows silent, and for one heartbeat the room isn't a room — Orion stands in a hall he has never seen, a figure at the far end already turning, already falling. He comes back with his ears ringing. A street-tough margin (rolled well under TN): the far flash lasts longer; he feels the Sire hit the floor. Near-miss (within 1): the ring dies true, but the snapback takes his hearing and voice for an hour — he goes through the rest of the vault season-deaf. Failure: the counter-note **loops** — the ring survives, the attempt is telegraphed up the thread, and the ledger flags high. No ring-warm, no tell; just time wearing a different shape thereafter.

**Branch B — bide.** No roll. Valerius can be heard from now on, and the session *keeps* being watched. The vault's contents — a scrawled stele of a skald's acoustic war-craft, "Bal's worm" named in stone — now stand in Valerius's ear as lore worth harvesting. He will want it. That is a lever the GM uses at once. And Bal's tutelage has a price of its own: learning to pull the leash is learning to *be* the leash.

## 5. DIALOGUE AUDIT (G6 — visible pass, run against Writing Guide §IX Rules 0 & 7 + brief §3 bans)

| Beat / line | Rule 0 — camera test (no narrated subtext) | Rule 7 — the *speaker's* want |
|---|---|---|
| **A** "These aren't letters. They're strokes…" | Pass — pure observation, filmable. | Speaker want: a reader locating herself in an unknown script; startled recognition seeking company. |
| **B** translation, incl. "It names the worm's master." | Pass — she is reading aloud; small stumble as the name lands (recoverable by action only, not a "she feels" clause). | Speaker want: to get the words right for the one man who needs them; the *name* slips out because accuracy matters more to her than caution. |
| **C** "Why this one and not its neighbors?" | Pass. | Speaker want: genuine apprentice curiosity — and a warning made in the only language the room allows. |
| **D** bridge peg, "nothing… rings by accident" | Pass. | Speaker want: to make the teacher look at what she saw; mild self-flagellation for touching it. |
| **E** near-slip, "We could cut it away tonight." | Pass — all action: hand on his wrist, ring turned to light. | Speaker want: hope, spoken straight — she is incapable of deceit, so the sentence is *earned by her nature*, and its danger is real. No reader-exposition here; she names nothing the ear hasn't already inferred. |
| **Cover line** "a snap travels the whole length…" | Pass — a lecture, filmable. | Speaker want: to amputate her sentence in public without amputating the friendship; to hand her the code. (Charged, not read: the line leaks one decodable fact to Valerius — that's a design cost, not a dialogue fault.) |
| **F** "What note did he leave singing?" | Pass. | Speaker want: to *receive* the answer from him — she has just learned the price of plainness and gives him the question she genuinely cannot answer. |
| **G** craft passage translation | Pass — literal document reading, no comprehension performed. | Speaker want: to finish the job of reading the room for him; she flags her own failure ("I don't know what it made") rather than covering it. |
| **Bal B1/B2/closers** | Pass — all lines are imperatives and prices; zero internal-state narration. | Speaker want: each line *buys* something — stop you (B1), arm you with the Soul Trap (B2), keep the offer warm regardless (closers). No line exists to inform the reader. |

**Register check (brief §3, G6):** no Nora lecture, no auto-solve, no mic-drops, nothing informing the reader through anyone's mouth, no ring-tell, no marrow-vibrations. The only truths spoken are documents read, observations made, and prices named.

### Lockout list (banned, regardless of how good they sound in the draft)

1. Nora explains sympathetic foci, counter-resonance, or unweaving — ever, in any draft.
2. Nora says "he's listening through the ring" or any equivalent line. She *learns* it by the interception; she never announces it.
3. The ring warms, pulses, or glows to flag Valerius's attention.
4. Bal described as vibration/marrow/prose. Cold, plain, commanding.
5. The stele contains the answer's *theory* — it contains a recipe, and the why is Orion's mouth.
6. Any invented stat value or fixed DC — modifiers only, applied to Orion's earned acoustic stat.
